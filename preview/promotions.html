through trusted networks.

We welcome considered introductions to investment opportunities, candid exchange with other family offices, and collaboration with capable, aligned partners.

How we connect

Sharing, opportunity, collaboration

There are no promotions or packages here. These are the conversations that are relevant to a private family office.

01

Investment opportunities

We welcome clear introductions to companies, funds, direct transactions, and other opportunities that may suit a long-term family mandate.

02

Family-office exchange

We value reciprocal dialogue with family-office peers around experience, perspective, and questions of shared relevance.

03

Specialist collaboration

We are open to relationships with investment, legal, tax, governance, and other specialists whose expertise may support our own mandate.

A culture of sharing

Exchange without turning insight into a sales pitch.

We believe family offices can learn from one another while respecting privacy, independence, and different mandates.

The aim is mutual perspective—not client acquisition, intermediation, or the sale of advisory products.

What earns attention

Relevance before volume.

We prefer a small number of well-framed introductions to a large flow of generic proposals.

Clear relevanceWhy the opportunity may fit a patient family-capital perspective.
Aligned peoplePrincipals with integrity, capability, and meaningful alignment.
Durable rationaleA clear source of value that can withstand thoughtful scrutiny.
Transparent contextUseful information on structure, risks, timing, and counterparties.
Making an introduction

A concise first note is enough.

Help us understand the opportunity and its relevance. Confidential or sensitive materials should only be shared after appropriate arrangements are in place.

  1. Who you areBriefly introduce the principals and your role.
  2. What it isSummarise the company, fund, transaction, or collaboration.
  3. Why MCMExplain why it may be relevant to our long-term perspective.
  4. What comes nextSet out timing and the most useful next step.
Start with context

Introduce an opportunity or propose an exchange.

We review relevant messages with discretion. An introduction does not imply interest, commitment, or an offer to transact.
