# MCM website design and content system

## Positioning

MCM Wealth Management Limited is presented as a **Hong Kong single family office investing its own family capital**. The website introduces MCM's perspective and the relationships it welcomes; it does not market retail or external wealth-management services.

Every future page should pass these checks:

1. Does the copy clearly speak in the first person about **our own** family mandate?
2. Could a visitor mistake the page for a service, package, promotion, public fund, or client-acquisition offer?
3. Is every factual or performance statement supported and approved?
4. Does the call to action invite a relevant opportunity, exchange, or collaboration rather than a sales consultation?

Do not publish investment-performance claims such as “successful track record” without verified figures, a defined measurement period, and legal/compliance approval. The approved public wording focuses on experience, long-term perspective, and openness to relevant opportunities.

## Messaging pillars

- **Family-led purpose** — continuity, accountability, and a multi-generational horizon.
- **Culture of sharing** — reciprocal knowledge exchange with trusted family-office peers; not paid advice.
- **Selective partnership** — openness to relevant opportunities and aligned founders, managers, co-investors, and specialists.
- **Independent judgment** — external expertise informs decisions; MCM retains responsibility for its own mandate.

Preferred calls to action include “Share an opportunity,” “Make an introduction,” “Open a dialogue,” and “Explore partnerships.” Avoid “Book a consultation,” “Choose a package,” “Claim an offer,” or language addressed to prospective wealth-management clients.

## Information architecture

- **Home** — identity, philosophy, and an overview of the family mandate.
- **About** — company facts, boundaries, values, and internal decision rhythm.
- **Investment Approach** — stewardship, capital allocation, continuity, enterprise, and specialist coordination for MCM's own capital.
- **Partnerships** — investment opportunities, family-office exchange, and specialist collaboration.
- **Contact** — routes relevant enquiries by purpose.

The legacy `services` and `promotions` URLs remain in place for backward compatibility, but their public labels and content are Investment Approach and Partnerships.

## Visual language

The visual system is restrained and editorial rather than retail or promotional.

### Core palette

- Ink: `#1B1815`
- Paper: `#F6F2EB`
- Surface: `#FFFDF9`
- Ember: `#C64F1A`
- Amber: `#DDA128`
- Line: `#DED4C6`

Warm ember and amber accents connect to the existing logo. The logo itself was not changed.

### Typography

- Display: Playfair Display
- Body and interface: Inter

Large editorial headings, concise eyebrow labels, fine borders, and generous spacing communicate privacy and permanence. Rounded pills are reserved for actions and compact metadata—not promotional badges.

### Imagery

Use calm, architectural, landscape, or still-life imagery that suggests patience, connection, stewardship, and Hong Kong context. Avoid staged adviser/client meetings, visible financial dashboards, currency, luxury clichés, trademarks, and identifiable people.

The current editorial image set was generated specifically for MCM and contains no third-party stock photography. See `ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`.

## Interaction and accessibility

- Keyboard-visible focus states and a skip link are required.
- Navigation must work at desktop and mobile widths and close with `Escape`.
- Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Images require descriptive alternative text unless decorative.
- English and Traditional Chinese copy must remain conceptually aligned; do not translate obsolete source copy.
- Contact forms must ask visitors not to submit confidential information in an initial message.

## Legal clarity

The footer and relevant pages state that MCM invests its own capital and that website content is not an offer, solicitation, recommendation, or professional advice. Introducing an opportunity does not create a client, advisory, fiduciary, or investment relationship and does not imply a commitment to transact.
